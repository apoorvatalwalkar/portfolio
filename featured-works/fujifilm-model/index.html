Background

Context

For this project, I was tasked with modeling and shading an item I owned, so I decided to model my own Fujifilm Polaroid camera. It had interesting geometry: overall it was really smooth, but with a lot of buttons and other protrusions, plus a decent amount of wear and tear to capture in the shading.

Getting Started

Taking Reference Photos

Since I owned the item, it was easy to set up my camera and take reference photos. To get a more orthographic look, I positioned myself further away from the object and zoomed in.

Step One

Modeling

After taking reference photos, I built the model. I started with a basic cube primitive, then continuously added edge loops and extruded faces until it resembled the reference. Once I was happy with the model, I edited the UV maps to make the shading step easier.

Key Insights

First Deep Dive

This was the first time I spent multiple weeks modeling and shading a single piece, rather than a quick one-off assignment.

From Reference to Geometry

Translating my own reference photos into 3D geometry, starting from a cube primitive and building up through edge loops and extrusions, was new territory for me.

Layered Shading

I picked up new techniques for layering shading and UV work to capture stickers, scuffs, and wear and tear realistically.

This was a very fruitful project, and I can't wait to do more.

Final

Results

The result is a fully shaded model of a Fujifilm camera spinning on a 360° turntable.
