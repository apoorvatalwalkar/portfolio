Context

Before this project, I had minimal experience with 3D modeling and animation. I'd made my first Maya model, a model of my room, a couple weeks prior, but had never modeled a character, built a rig, or animated anything. Although this was a large project to take on at the level I was at, I believed the best way to learn was to dive straight in and do it.

Step One

Character Design and Modeling

The first step was designing the character. I modeled my base character after myself, then moved on to modeling in 3D, using a mix of techniques including starting from a primitive, quad drawing, and sculpting to build up the figure.

Step Two

Rigging and Walk Cycle

Once my character was modeled and textured, I moved on to rigging and animating a walk cycle. I used a basic Human IK rig to set up the skeleton, then keyframe animation to create a looping walk.

I worked with weight painting to get more natural movement, especially in the clothing and hair. It took a few iterations to go from a jittery initial walk to a smoother final one.

The looping walk cycle, from rig to final motion

Step Three

Environment

Lastly, I built the environment for my character to walk through. I chose Wayland Arch, the dorm I lived in sophomore year at Brown, which has distinctive architecture and made for a good setting. This step gave me my first real experience working with skydomes and additional lights for a more realistic scene, and my first time texturing anything, including the building, the trees, and my character's face.

Results & Takeaways

This project pushed me to learn as much as I could about 3D modeling and animation in a short, four-week timeline, and I'm proud of what I put together in that time. It opened my eyes to what's possible with 3D animation and what it takes to get to realistic, high-fidelity results.

If I continued this project, I'd work more on the character's textures to make them more lifelike. I'm looking forward to doing more in Maya going forward.
